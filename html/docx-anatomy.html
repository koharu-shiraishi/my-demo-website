Where's My Familiar Icon?

You append .zip to the end of the file name. A popup warns you that the file might become unusable if you change its format. You hesitate for a second, then click through anyway — you've come this far. The popup disappears, and so does the familiar blue "W" icon. In its place: a plain, grey icon — the same one you've seen before, on folders of photos someone compressed before emailing them to you. A question forms in your mind: "Why did my document just turn into one of those zipped folders? What is actually inside it?"

Opening the Folder

You right-click the file. Windows 11 shows a short menu of icons, with "Show more options" at the bottom. There, you find "Extract All...". It asks where to save the result; you accept the default. A new folder appears, named exactly like your original document. You open it, expecting to find... your document, somehow. Instead, you find more folders with names like word and docProps, and inside them, files with long names ending in letters you don't recognize. Nothing here looks like a document. You're not looking for a fix anymore. You just want to know what you're looking at.

Asking for a Second Opinion

You scroll back to the chat box under that AI summary from earlier. You press the attach button, upload a screenshot of the folder, and type: "What is all this? I just renamed a Word file to .zip and extracted it."

The reply explains what you're looking at: this is the internal structure of your .docx file. The files you don't recognize end in .xml — a format for storing structured text, used by this and many other kinds of files. One of them, word/document.xml, holds your actual words.

You've never heard of XML before, so you ask: "What is an XML file? I've never seen one." The AI explains: it's a plain text format, readable by both computers and humans, that uses tags to describe pieces of content — similar in spirit to HTML, the format behind most web pages. You open document.xml anyway, out of curiosity. Your sentences are in there, just as the AI said — buried in a single, unbroken wall of text, between tags like <w:r> and <w:rPr>. One more question comes to mind, and you type it into the same chat: "Why does it have to be built like this?"
