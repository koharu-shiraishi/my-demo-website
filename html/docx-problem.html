The day your document broke

Have you ever had this experience? You spend hours editing a document in Microsoft Word or WPS — styling text, adjusting the layout, polishing every detail. You save it as .docx (or .doc, if your version of Word is older than 2007) and send it to a friend or coworker, hoping for a good response. Instead, you get a message asking what is wrong with your file, along with a screenshot of the document as it appears on their computer: a messy layout you spent hours perfecting, unexpected fonts, broken alignment, white rivers running through the text.

Why does the file break on other computers?

You reopen the file on your own computer, and it still renders exactly as you intended. So what happened? Here is the surprising part: your file is not corrupted, and your friend's computer is not broken. The document simply does not look the same everywhere, because a .docx file does not fully describe how it should look. To understand why, we need to look at what is actually inside the file.

This trick only works for .docx. The older .doc format (Word 97–2003) is a completely different, binary format — not a ZIP archive at all, and not human-readable by any trick this simple.
